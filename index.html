A planning tool built on Lester, “Rational Foreclosure” (2026)

The Hope Gap

When the things people work toward pull away from their paychecks, many stop measuring themselves against them. They keep their jobs, but stop grinding. This tool estimates how many people in your jurisdiction have reached that point, how many will over the next two years, and what someone in your office can do about it.

Step 5

The plan

The target

Help those still in the race first. People near the edge are still trying; a small improvement keeps them in. People already past the quitting point have stopped keeping score, and pushing them to persist adds to their losses.

Slow the benchmark, or speed up pay. Those are the two levers that move the quitting point. Damping the benchmark's swings alone can push people near the edge out sooner, so pair any stabilizing measure with slower growth.

Measure it like unemployment. The inputs are published monthly for the nation and quarterly for states. Re-run this every quarter and report the share against the target.

What is under the hood

Data and methods

How the numbers become the model's inputs

Pulling away. The drift gap is the benchmark's growth minus pay growth over the chosen period. In the model, people's resources grow with pay when they make no extra sacrifice; the race's extra saving is the grind.

Jumpiness. For housing, the standard deviation of yearly changes in the Case-Shiller national index, 1990 to 2026 (5.9%), or of the nearest metro index for a state. For prices, about 1.5% (CPI, 1990 to 2026). For output per person, about 2%.

Standing. Housing: three times income divided by the home value (a home costing three years of income is par). Prices: real median weekly pay against 2019. Growth: the ratio of real median pay to real GDP per person, against 1979.

The group. Age changes standing through income by age of householder (ACS 2019 to 2023: under 25, $43,534; 25 to 44, $87,575; 45 to 64, $94,847; 65 and over, $57,108), unless an income tier is chosen, which then sets income directly (2025 Census percentiles: 20th, $35,800; 60th, $110,300; 80th, $182,400). The directions of the preference adjustments come from the cited studies; their sizes are assumptions of this tool, shown in the table below and adjustable through the preference menus.

Sensitivity. Where the benchmark jumps around little, as home prices do nationally, the quitting point moves quickly with the trend: at U.S. housing values it rises from about 50% to about 66% of par as the gap goes from 1.0 to 1.3 points a year. The results report how today's share changes if the trend is a quarter point off.

The simulation. Today's group is spread around its typical standing; each member's standing then evolves month by month with the benchmark's drift and swings, and anyone who reaches the quitting point stops for good. The scenario changes the drift, the swings, or both, from today. The target is the largest drift gap at which the share who have stopped would rise by no more than half a point over the horizon.

Limits. This is one decision, solved exactly, applied to a group. It takes prices and pay as given, so it cannot show how disengagement feeds back into them. Population counts use national age shares within states and localities and treat household income tiers as shares of people. Read changes and comparisons more than levels.

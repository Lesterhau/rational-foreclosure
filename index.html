Your office's part

    

    
Guardrails. Do not answer this with campaigns urging people past the quitting point to try harder: in the model, holding someone in past that point costs them more than the race is worth. One-time help with buying raises prices unless more homes come with it. Calming price swings alone can push people near the edge out sooner, because swings are part of what keeps their hope alive.

The tipping point

    
When the goal pulls away from pay faster than its normal ups and downs can make up for, people start to stop. Below that tipping point, the chance that the gap closes on its own is worth waiting for, and no one has a reason to quit. Above it, the quitting point climbs fast. That distance above the tipping point is the Hope Gap.

How the numbers become the model's inputs

      
The gap. The goal's growth minus pay growth over the chosen period, in percentage points a year. Home prices: FHFA House Price Index (counties: FHFA's annual county index). Pay: average weekly wages of all workers covered by unemployment insurance (BLS Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages), the broadest measure of local pay. Cost of living: the Consumer Price Index.

      
The tipping point and the Hope Gap. In the model, people have a reason to stop only when the gap is larger than the square of the goal's typical yearly swing. That swing is the standard deviation of yearly changes in the home price index since 1990 for the area (for cost of living, of yearly changes in real pay). The Hope Gap is the gap minus the tipping point. It does not depend on any assumption about people's preferences.

      
Standing. Owning a home: three years of household income divided by the typical home value (Zillow Home Value Index, or the Census median where Zillow has none), so 100% means a typical home costs three years of income. Each household's standing follows its income: incomes in the group are spread as in the area's Census income distribution, shifted for the group's age, race and ethnicity, education and housing status using Census medians, and limited to the income levels or tax brackets chosen. Cost of living: local pay against its 2019 level, divided by consumer prices against theirs.

      
Who. Head counts multiply the area's adults in the chosen ages by the shares of the chosen sex, race and ethnicity, education, housing status and income range. These shares are treated as independent, so counts for narrow groups are approximate.

      
People. How much falling behind stings and how much people weigh today over the future are set from the chosen ages, weighted by population; the table below lists the values. The directions come from the cited studies; the sizes are this tool's assumptions.

      
The simulation. Two thousand people spread across the group's standing move month by month with the goal's trend and swings. Those below the quitting point have stopped. Scenarios and plans change the actual trend at once or over two years; people's sense of the trend catches up at the pace chosen in Step 3, and the quitting point moves with it. Someone who has stopped starts again only if the quitting point falls below where they now stand.

      
Plans. Closing the gap means bringing the goal's growth down, or pay growth up, by the Hope Gap. Homes needed use a long-run price elasticity of housing demand of about −0.6 (Hanushek and Quigley 1980): each extra 1% of homes lowers prices about 1.7%; the range uses −0.4 to −1.0. Pay plans are priced as a credit that grows by the needed share of the group's median income each year. Help for people near the edge lifts their standing by 10%. Tax brackets are 2026 brackets for married couples filing jointly, converted to household income by adding the $32,200 standard deduction; single filers' dollar ranges are about half.

      
Limits. This is one decision, solved exactly, applied to a group. It takes prices and pay as given, so it cannot show how disengagement feeds back into them. The quitting point moves fast near the tipping point, so the share who have stopped is less certain than the Hope Gap; the results show how much it changes if the trend is off by a quarter point. Read changes and comparisons more than levels.
